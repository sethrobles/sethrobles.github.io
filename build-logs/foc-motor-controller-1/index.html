The development of the FOC Motor Controller (after the end of 2.679)

While taking 2.679 my junior spring, I began the development of an FOC motor controller based on ODrive 3.5 (the last open source one I believe). I didn't bookmark the specific schematic I used for reference, but there's a chance that it was this one. It helped a lot for my understanding in the moment, but because of the rushes of Sin Limite around the time I was supposed to be designing the board, I ended up speed running it in a day and did not rigorously check the components that I was using. If I attach the photo of the PCB then you'll see. It was all over the place.

My goal here is to actually develop a board which is able to drive two motors, but this time do so more rigorously and keep better documentation. As a result, here are the starting goals:

  • Power: 36V bus, 10A Continuous, 30A Peak (Per motor ?)
  • Flash: CAN bootloader


Here is the running list of updates goals

  • Power: 18-40V input bus (24, 36 nominal), 10A continouous, 30A peak
  • Communication: CAN2 bus for communication to board + bootloading, SPI to encoders


So my first step is going to be deciding on a microcontroller. My previous design used the STM32G474RBT6, but I will be moving towards the STM32G474RET6. The difference is that the new one has 512KB flash as opposed to the 128KB of the first. This will cost around 2 dollars more, but it benefits from the fact that I will not be pressed on the flash storage. Well this is partially verified by Claude since I'm not sure what's a normal amount of storage. Regardless, for this first design, I'd prefer to be safe rather than sorry. There's a design with 256KB flash that I might someday be interested in for a more open source, low-cost board, but I think this will do for now.

I just wanted to start this build log, my goal for the next few days is to 1) verify the drivers I'll get, 2) decide on the structure of the power stage (likely a buck converter stepping down the bus for the microcontroller, and 3) decide on any other goals that I'd like to add.

Power Stage

`

For the power stage, I want it to be compatible with 24 and 36V, so I'll widen up the range. This will mostly affect the buck converter sizing that I will go with now. I'm thinking that we'll make it work with an 18-40V input bus + have capacity for any transient spikes from regenerative voltage differences (or anything else I might not be foreseeing).

I am currently debating whether I want a 3.3V or 5V CAN bus. I think the 3.# removes the extra requirement, which means I don't need the extra part of an LDO + the STM32 can be directly wired up to the CAN without shifting logic levels. For now, I am going to go with this. I'll note in the future that maybe it should go up. Here is a table with my current power estimates*:

*Thank you Claude for the table, we might need to work on it though.

Given this, I'm going to start looking into buck converters that will be able to bring us down from the bus to the 3.3V that will be used for all of these. I'm going to use a 1.5x safety factor from the 40V input bus, so we'll say a buck rated for 60V and for current, we'll use a 2x safety factor and rate it for 500 mA. This is likely far more than it needs, but I'd prefer to be safe than sorry. A future iteration could potentially use a cheaper buck.

Now I'm searching for a buck. These are the few that I am considering with help from Claude again. I previously used from TI and made a terrible mistake while using their Power Designer. I clicked on the first item and did not check the nature of the buck. It was a prototype that I centered my design around and I spent a decent amount of time trying to fix it. I would verify that whatever you choose, it's well stocked by whatever supplier you're using.

July 10 2026

For sizing capacitance, I have done a little bit of reading. I've typically gone off suggestions in the past / other designs, but now I'm trying to be more deliberate.

So for sizing the bus cap bank, I'll be estimating the power draw.

Given 10A continouous per bus, we'll have an RMS rating of 10 / sqrt(2) = 7.41A. This means we'll have an expected 14.82A expected (round to 15). We can then size for half of this (not fully sure why), but I will need to find enough capacitors with >= 60V that are able to add up to this 7A requirement for ripple current.

Additionally, I'll need to verify that it works for any potential voltage sag. We can find the expected voltage sag with this equation: Delta V ≈ I·D/(f_sw·C).

Here, current is the max current draw, f_sw is the frequency of switching (driven by our PWM frequency), C is the capacitance (set up above) and D is the duty cycle. At the worst case, duty cycle is at its maximum, which occurs at 50%.
