FOC Motor Controller

pcb-design

While taking 2.679 my junior spring, I began the development of an FOC motor controller based on ODrive 3.5 (the last open source one I believe). I didn't bookmark the specific schematic I used for reference, but there's a chance that it was this one. It helped a lot for my understanding in the moment, but because of the rushes of Sin Limite around the time I was supposed to be designing the board, I ended up speed running it in a day and did not rigorously check the components that I was using. If I attach the photo of the PCB then you'll see. It was all over the place.

My goal here is to actually develop a board which is able to drive two motors, but this time do so more rigorously and keep better documentation. As a result, here are the starting goals:

  • Power: 36V bus, 10A Continuous, 30A Peak (Per motor ?)
  • Flash: CAN bootloader


Here is the running list of updates goals

  • Power: 20-40V input bus (24, 36 nominal), 10A continouous, 30A peak
  • Communication: CAN2 bus for communication to board + bootloading, SPI to encoders


So my first step is going to be deciding on a microcontroller. My previous design used the STM32G474RBT6, but I will be moving towards the STM32G474RET6. The difference is that the new one has 512KB flash as opposed to the 128KB of the first. This will cost around 2 dollars more, but it benefits from the fact that I will not be pressed on the flash storage. Well this is partially verified by Claude since I'm not sure what's a normal amount of storage. Regardless, for this first design, I'd prefer to be safe rather than sorry. There's a design with 256KB flash that I might someday be interested in for a more open source, low-cost board, but I think this will do for now.

I just wanted to start this build log, my goal for the next few days is to 1) verify the drivers I'll get, 2) decide on the structure of the power stage (likely a buck converter stepping down 36V for the microcontroller, and 3) decide on any other goals that I'd like to add.

For the power stage, I want it to be compatible with 24 and 36V, so I'll widen up the range. This will mostly affect the buck converter sizing that I will go with now.
